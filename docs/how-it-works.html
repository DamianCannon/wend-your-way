How Wend Your Way Works

A personal planner for running, gravel and trail-bike routes in England and Wales that favours footpaths, alleys and cut-throughs over roads with traffic. It's measured against Trail Router: from the same starts, its Loops should be at least as good to your eye. Everything runs on one laptop; only the map pictures come from outside.

Six jobs, three drawers

Each part does one job. The database keeps the map, the place names and your history in separate drawers, so rebuilding the map from fresh OpenStreetMap data never touches your runs, stars or Saved Routes.

The mapmaker

wend network build

Turns OpenStreetMap into Segments, the stretch of a way between two junctions. Labels each one's ground and who may use it (on foot, by bike, on a trail bike), spots pavements beside roads, marks greenery, water, Corridors, Crossings, gates and stiles, draws straight crossings over open land, gives every Segment its climbing, joins green ground into Off-road networks and finds the car parks beside them.

Great Britain: about 4 hours to rebuild

The archivist

wend outings import-strava

Reads your Strava export: runs, walks, hikes and rides; swims and trainer rides are skipped. Works out which Segments each Outing covered and where it left the map, then follows it onto the map and scores it as a Loop is, for the app's stars.

1,359 Outings; 41 too far off the map to rate

The gazetteer

wend places import

Loads Ordnance Survey's place names and postcodes, so the search box can take the map anywhere in England and Wales without asking any outside service.

under a minute

The route finder

Core, plain C#

Takes the nearby part of the map into memory and builds Loops out to the best green places within reach and through points round your start, joining them with its own A*. Cuts out spurs, judges each whole Loop as you would, leans to fewer or more hills as you ask, and offers up to five that head different ways.

10 km: a few seconds · 100 km: under 30 s

The shop window

The web app

Blazor in your browser, on this laptop only. Takes your start (a click, a car park, a place or postcode), Distance and Activity; shows each Loop with its Breakdown, profile and a sentence or two of what it's like; lets you nudge it, save it, and hand it on as GPX, TCX or a QR code.

http://127.0.0.1:5018

The courier

To phone

When you press QR, a listener of its own starts on your Wi-Fi and serves that one route, at a random link, once, for ten minutes. The app itself never leaves the laptop.

then Footpath, OS Maps or Komoot

Three Activities

Each Loop is found for one Activity, which decides which ways it may use and how it's scored.

Run

On foot: footpaths, alleys, cut-throughs, parks and open ground; roads only to join the good bits.

Gravel Ride

By bike: bridleways, byways, tracks and cycle paths; not footpaths or steps. A path with no word on bikes is used but flagged Unconfirmed. Gates and stiles counted.

Trail Ride

On a road-legal trail bike: roads, and byways open to all traffic from the councils' Definitive Maps; never a footpath or bridleway. Green lanes the map can't confirm are flagged. Every one says to check for closures first.

A Loop, described

Open a Loop's card and it tells you what it's like before the numbers: its feel, where it goes, and where it climbs.

A green, gently rolling 12.8 km by the water. It sets off beside the River Lee, through Walthamstow Wetlands, and finishes along traffic-free paths.

Its exported GPX and TCX mark each turn worth saying, at a junction with a choice where it turns 45° or more: "Left onto Bush Road", "Right onto the footpath". About 3½ a km.

Words you'll see

Segment
The stretch of one way between two junctions; the smallest piece a Loop is made of.
Off-road
Unsurfaced and free of traffic: field paths, bridleways, tracks, woodland trails.
Traffic-free
Paved but no cars: alleys, cut-throughs, park paths, towpaths.
Connector
Roads, and pavements beside them: the glue between the good bits.
Main Road
A busy road, or the pavement beside one: only ever a short link.
Open ground
Straight crossings of parks, commons and heath, never through a fence, wall, railway or stream.
Off-road network
Green ground joined up across short road links: how long a Loop can stay off roads there.
Trailhead
A public car park beside a large Off-road network: the car parks listed around here.
Unconfirmed
A way the map can't say this Activity may use: used, but flagged "check this".
Nudge
Dragging a Loop's line to where it should go, or avoiding a bit of it: only the stretch round it changes.
Outing
One of your own runs, walks or rides, imported from Strava or a GPX file.
Gap
Where an Outing went more than 25 m from any way on the map: open ground, GPS astray, or a path OSM is missing.
Route gap
Where a route made from a track couldn't be matched onto the map; the ways may be there.
Stars
The app's, from its score, each a fifth of your Outings; and yours, given with a click. Two or more apart, and it stands out.
Turn point
A junction with a choice where a route turns 45° or more, in words, in the GPX and the TCX.

How a Loop is judged

Your verdicts on Trail Router's Loops, and on Wend Your Way's from the same starts, set these. The route finder steers by stricter costs than the judge, so it keeps off Main Roads while a whole Loop isn't marked down for a short link to somewhere good.

Counts for a LoopCounts against it
Off-road and Traffic-free groundRoads: 3× a path's cost a metre
Greenery: up to 30% off a metre beside parks and woodsEach Knot: 15% off the score
Corridors: 20% off a metre moreGround covered again: 2× its cost
Flow: up to half as much againCrossings: 20 to 300 m each
Water: up to half as much againMain Roads, to the router: 15× a path
Hills, as you lean: climbing per km lifts the score, or lowers itFlat ground up to 2.5× (more hills); steep up to 2× (fewer)

Hills

Every Segment knows how much it climbs, so you can ask for fewer hills or more, and see the Loop's ups and downs before you go.

The Hills slider

FewerMore

Leaning to more hills makes flat ground dearer; leaning to fewer makes steep ground dearer. Nothing ever gets cheaper, so Loops stay green.

The commands

wend is the command line: dotnet run -c Release --project src/Wend Your Way.Cli -- <command>. Every command takes --database <connection string>; without it, the app's own database on this laptop. Ctrl+C stops any of them cleanly: a build leaves the map as it was, an import or scoring keeps what it finished.

CommandWhat it doesOptions
network build <extract>Loads an OpenStreetMap extract (.osm.pbf) and builds the whole Network from it, swapping it in when done; your Outings are covered and scored again.--osm2pgsql where osm2pgsql is (default: the copy in tools/); --terrain the OS Terrain 50 tiles (default data/terrain50/asc); --rights-of-way the byways (default data/rights-of-way)
network rebuildThe same, from the extract already loaded: after a rule changes, or new heights or byways.--terrain, --rights-of-way
network offroadFinds the Off-road networks, car parks, gates and stiles and Gravel hotspots again, in minutes.
outings import-strava <zip>Your whole Strava history; a newer export adds only what's new.
outings import-gpx <file or folder>Loose GPX, TCX or FIT files, the same way.
outings scoreThe app's stars for Outings not scored yet.--all every Outing again, after a scoring change
places import <folder>OS Open Names' CSV, in place of what was there.

Keeping the data fresh

Your own data lives apart from the map, linked by OpenStreetMap's own ids, so refreshing any of these never loses your Outings, stars, Saved Routes or Marks.

DataChangesTo refresh
OpenStreetMapDaily; monthly is plenty, or after you've fixed something in OSMDownload great-britain-latest.osm.pbf (about 2 GB) from download.geofabrik.de into data/, then wend network build it: about 30 minutes to load and 4 hours to build. Best overnight.
Definitive Map bywaysA few times a yearscripts/get-rights-of-way.ps1 (fetches only what changed), then wend network rebuild.
OS Open NamesQuarterlyDownload the CSV for Great Britain from osdatahub.os.uk, unzip it, then wend places import: under a minute.
OS Terrain 50Yearly, in JulyUnzip the new tiles into data/terrain50/asc, then wend network rebuild.
Your runsWhenever you likeA new bulk export from Strava, then wend outings import-strava: only new Outings are added, covered and scored.

A rule changed, not the data? wend network rebuild is enough. Only how car parks or Off-road networks are found? wend network offroad. Only the scoring? wend outings score --all.
